## Decision

This inventory defines a reproducible first-wave acquisition set of complete, static federal documents from canonical government endpoints. It also records official living guides and state exemplars that require a separate capture or rights decision.

"All government style manuals" is not a finite, stable collection. Federal agencies, military services, courts, legislatures, states, and local governments maintain independently versioned manuals and living web guides. This report therefore means:

1. the common federal baseline;
2. official drafting manuals for legislation and regulations;
3. representative executive, judicial, scientific, correspondence, military, digital, plain-language, and accessibility guides;
4. the current federal agency index maintained by GSA; and
5. a bounded set of state exemplars, with state rights kept separate from federal rights.

The first wave contains 18 source artifacts. A source may enter HammerTime only if its bytes resolve from its recorded official endpoint, its MIME type matches the expected type, and its artifact-level rights review does not identify protected third-party material. Any failure is quarantined, not silently omitted or replaced by a mirror.

## Authority and conflict hierarchy

The corpus must preserve scope and conflict rather than produce a single universal rule set.

1. Court rules, statutes, regulations, and a document-issuing authority's mandatory instructions control within their own scope.
2. The Office of the Federal Register Document Drafting Handbook controls Federal Register format and codification. Its regulatory drafting and incorporation-by-reference guidance applies within those functions.
3. A current agency or chamber guide controls that issuing body's work when it conflicts with a general guide.
4. The GPO Style Manual supplies the general federal printing and publication baseline when a controlling authority has not provided a more specific rule.
5. Federal plain-language guidance adds audience, organization, clarity, and usability principles unless a controlling document rule requires a different treatment.
6. USWDS and agency digital guides govern web presentation and accessibility, not legislative text, court filings, or Federal Register codification.
7. State manuals apply only to their own jurisdiction. They must never be represented as federal rules.

The Supreme Court states that it does not publish a style manual for advocates. Its Rules and Guides for Counsel are filing requirements and must be modeled as such, not mislabeled as a general style manual.

## Rights rule

The working presumption for a work prepared by a United States Government employee as part of official duties is 17 U.S.C. 105. That presumption is not enough by itself. Federal websites and manuals may contain contractor, licensed, quoted, photographic, font, logo, or other third-party content. Each acquired artifact therefore receives an embedded-rights scan and an artifact-level decision.

State and local works are not covered by 17 U.S.C. 105. Public access is not a reuse license. State manuals remain deferred until their issuing jurisdiction's applicable rights terms are captured and reviewed.

## Sources excluded from canonical acquisition

- Unofficial mirrors of the Senate Legislative Drafting Manual, Army AR 25-50, CIA Style Manual, or any other government work are discovery leads only.
- Commercial stylebooks, dictionaries, fonts, and standards referenced by an official manual are not acquired under the government's rights status.
- Superseded editions are not treated as current rules. They may enter a separate historical corpus only with explicit version labels.
- Search-result text, third-party summaries, and this research report are not substitutes for source artifacts and do not enter HammerTime as semantic authority.
- A living site is not considered completely captured merely because its landing page was downloaded.

## Source-observed core principles

These observations guide the later Qwen3.8 analysis but are not a semantic corpus artifact:

1. Identify the controlling authority and document type before applying any style rule.
2. Write for the intended reader, put the major point first, and use clear organization, headings, lists, and tables.
3. Prefer direct, concrete, concise language and active voice where the legal or factual meaning permits it.
4. Preserve defined terms, legal effects, technical precision, exceptions, and modal force. Clarity does not authorize changing substance.
5. Treat typography, white space, headings, tables, figures, and accessible structure as part of meaning and usability.
6. Apply document-specific settings for margins, fonts, numbering, signatures, citations, filing formats, and production requirements.
7. Verify quotations, citations, cross-references, names, dates, numbers, and authority locators against the exact source.
8. Label rules as mandatory, recommended, example, historical, superseded, or uncertain rather than flattening their force.
9. Record conflicts and the rule's jurisdiction, issuing body, version, and locator.
10. Keep third-party rights and external-source verification separate from government-source provenance.

- All 18 first-wave URLs use the recorded canonical government endpoint.

- A PDF must begin with the PDF signature and pass structural inspection.
- Duplicate bytes are recorded explicitly and never silently copied twice.
- Changed bytes on a repeat run stop the acquisition until reviewed.

- Inaccessible official endpoints stay deferred. No mirror substitution is permitted.
- The manifest and source-root contain only the owned batch.
- All generated task artifacts use ASCII hyphens.

## Known limitations and next discovery work

- The federal agency index is broad but not guaranteed to enumerate every internal or newly published guide.
- Many current digital guides are living, multi-page websites and need a versioned crawler with bounded traversal and link manifests.
- Several official pages expose documents through anti-bot or JavaScript delivery. Those sources need an approved capture path, not a bypass or mirror.
- State rights vary and need jurisdiction-specific review.
- Local-government manuals were outside this first bounded inventory.
- Currentness must be rechecked before any profile is used as a controlling rule.
